Research Focus

I am a developmental neuroscientist and stem-cell biologist building human, patient-relevant models of the developing brain to uncover disease mechanisms and advance therapeutics. My program spans the genetic and environmental determinants of neuronal development and translational drug discovery.

Modeling environmental and genetic disorders of the developing brain in human organoids. Using human brain organoids and forebrain assembloids, I model how neuronal migration and maturation are disrupted by environmental insults (hypoxic-ischemic injury of prematurity) and genetic risk (22q11.2 deletion syndrome). I live-imaged interneuron migration under hypoxia and defined mitochondrial fragmentation, impaired oxidative phosphorylation, and dysregulated calcium signaling in 22q11.2DS interneurons, identifying adrenomedullin (ADM) as a common rescue acting through PKA/DRP1 and PLC/calcium pathways.

Engineering next-generation human brain organoid models (NAMs). I develop orthogonally regulated, multi-lineage brain-region organoids and forebrain assembloids (the patent-pending ORChID + INVIGOR platform) that recreate the neuron–glia, neuron–microglia, and neuron–vasculature milieu of the human fetal and neonatal brain for disease modeling and therapeutic testing.

Novel therapeutics: adrenomedullin (ADM) analogs. I am advancing ADM-analog peptide therapeutics from organoid-based discovery toward the clinic (issued and pending patents), and building high-throughput screening pipelines to identify additional neuroprotective compounds.

Previously: cytoskeletal regulation of neural circuit development. My doctoral work established the Formin Fmn2/Fmn2b as a regulator of axon outgrowth, branching, and circuit assembly in zebrafish, and generated the first CRISPR Fmn2b mutants as a community resource.
